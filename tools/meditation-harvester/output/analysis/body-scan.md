# Body scan

**What the sources share.** Primary body scans (the clinical-style ones, the Mark Williams one, the VA one, the short guided ones) all follow the same logic: settle, anchor on breath, sweep attention through the body in sequence, widen to the whole body, close with rest and a return. Teachers use "what's here?" questions, "no right way to feel", "as best you can", and a "breathe into, let go on the out-breath" handoff between regions.

**Where they differ.** Direction runs feet-up or head-down. Granularity runs from toe-by-toe (about 30–45 min) to regional (10 min). Flavour varies: compassion/kindness versions, healing-breath versions, relaxation-suggestion versions ("heavy, loose, limp"), and a children's imagery version.

**Phases.** Opening and permission (about 10–15%), breath anchor (about 10%), the scan itself (about 55–65%), whole-body widening and rest (about 10–15%), return (about 5%). Longer sessions slow the scan and add steps within regions rather than new phases.

**Poor fits.** Several sources are really other styles: the iRest and yoga nidra pieces with opposites and "I" inquiry, the 61-point numbered nidra, the space-awareness piece, the autogenic phrases, the affirmations/depression piece, the walking talk, the imagery/hypnosis count-down, and the sleep-induction piece. I weighted these low.

**Pauses.** Median typical pause is about 2.5s. Short pauses of 1–3s follow cues within a region. Longer silences of 10–20s follow a region's invitation to feel. The longest silences (30s and up) fall at the whole-body rest and the close. Silence is slightly longer mid-scan than at the start. Some sources have very long trailing silences that are just padding.
