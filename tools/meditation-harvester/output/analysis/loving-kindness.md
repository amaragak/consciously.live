# Loving-kindness

Most sources share one engine: three or four short wishes repeated silently, aimed at one recipient at a time. The typical order is self, a benefactor or loved one, a neutral person, a difficult person, then all beings. Some start with a loved one so the feeling comes easily. A few run in widening rings (room, town, country, planet). Intros are mostly posture, eyes closed or soft gaze, breath or body settling, and a brief naming of the practice and phrases.

Where they differ:
- Wish-line spacing: tight call-and-response (about 2–4s between lines) versus a phrase given once, then long silence (30–130s).
- Teaching preamble: lecture-heavy versus none.
- Imagery: light and luminosity, or being gazed at kindly, versus pure phrase focus.
- Closing: eyes open and carry into the day, or silence.

Poor fits for this style:
- The forgiveness practice, the body scan, the shame practice and the emotion-soothing practice belong with self-compassion.
- The Dispenza track is garbled heart-focus, not metta.
- Some Salzberg uploads are mostly lecture.

Phases are recipient blocks. Each has a cue, a few phrase rounds, then silence. Sessions grow by adding recipients or longer silence rather than by adding new techniques.

Pauses: 2–4s between wish lines. The first block after the opening runs longest. Silence grows after each recipient cue, with a long final silence. Mid-session phrase pauses stay short; the long gaps come after a recipient is named, to let the wishes land. Closing silence is often a minute or more.
