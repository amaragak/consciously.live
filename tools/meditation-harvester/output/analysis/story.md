# Story

All four are told stories; none is a conventional meditation. Three are sleep stories (a travelogue, a comic pirate tale, a quiet first-person domestic piece) and one is a kids' yoga story with a short body and breath bookend. Cosmic Kids is the most "meditation-like": it has a settling opening, a story with an embedded lesson, then a body-relaxation close. Nothing Much Happens is closest to pure sleep-story craft, with deliberately uneventful content and a full second telling at a slower pace.

Shared features:
- A short welcome and framing.
- Settling cues (breaths, getting comfortable) before the tale.
- Concrete, sensory narration in short clauses, with one detail per breath.
- Low drama and gentle humour.
- A soft landing into sleep at the end.

Differences:
- Person: second-person "you" (travelogue) vs first-person narrator (pirate, journal piece) vs third-person character (dragon).
- Plot density: the pirate tale has a quest; the others are nearly plotless.
- Repetition: only the quiet piece repeats, as a whole second telling.
- Explicit instructions: almost none beyond the opening and close.

Phases: framing and settling, then story (arrival, unfolding, winding down), then a drift-off or return.

Pauses: most are 1–2s, placed at clause breaks. Pause length is steady throughout. The longest silence is at the very end, as sleep space. Isolated mid-story gaps (5–10s) mark a breath or image. The quiet piece uses longer pauses (2–4s) throughout.
