# Manifestation

**What the sources share:** The listener is relaxed first, then asked to experience a desired outcome as already real, with emphasis on feeling it in the body. Most end by releasing or surrendering the wish, then gratitude. Certainty is high. Language is mostly "you", often as-if-true or future-certain ("it is already on its way").

**Variants:**
- Scripted visualization: sensory scene, emotion, anchoring.
- Dispenza-style: long silences after short cues, with energy or space-awareness work before the future feeling.
- Abraham-style: statement, "breathe in, breathe out", statement, in a very regular rhythm.
- Mantra or sound formats: a long explanation, then a repeated phrase and a very long silence.
- Hypnosis countdowns, plus lists of affirmations.

**Phases:**
1. Welcome, settling, and sometimes an intention.
2. Relaxation or deepening, by body scan, breath, a countdown, or light flowing down.
3. Future scene or feeling, built through senses, then emotion, then embodying it.
4. Anchoring through affirmation, gratitude, or a mantra.
5. Surrender or release.
6. Return.

**Poor fits:**
- Two sources are almost all explanation, with the practice carried by music.
- The Abraham morning piece is a pure monologue.
- The walking meditation is movement-based.
- Several Dispenza transcripts are garbled, so their phrasing is unreliable.

**Pauses:** The median pause is about 3s. Pauses after cues that ask the listener to imagine or feel run 10–40s. Long silences of 60s to several minutes sit in the middle or late on the future-feeling or surrender. Pauses are shorter in the final tenth, near the return.
