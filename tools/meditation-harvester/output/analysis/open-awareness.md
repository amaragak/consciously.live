# Open awareness

**What the sources share.** Open awareness here means a short, plain anchor (posture, breath, sounds), then a widening to "whatever arises," then a pointer to the awareness that knows it. Noting is near-universal. Thoughts, sounds and sensations are treated as arising and passing "like clouds" or "weather." Very little imagery is used beyond sky, space, mirror and ocean.

**Where they differ.**
- One camp is the Harris-style lineage: concrete cues, repeated "come back to the breath" loops, and experiments such as recalling a thought or opening the eyes.
- A second camp is non-dual inquiry: "Am I aware?", "Who am I?", and resting as being.
- A third is the quiet-listening lineage: allowing, stillness, and letting go of the meditator.
- A fourth is the "head to heart" shift of awareness.
- Many sources are teaching talks wrapped around a short meditation.

**Phases.** Sessions run roughly like this: settle and ground, anchor, widen, point to the knower, rest in long silence, and a brief return. Longer sessions add inquiry blocks and silence rather than new technique.

**Poor fits.** The recitation-of-sutras session, the long manifestation-style session, and the "future personality" session are only loosely open awareness. The anxiety surrender session and the "exploring difficulty" session are really feeling-focused. The Chopra-inspired session is overlong and nearly continuous, with pauses of about 1.2s throughout.

**Pauses.** Pauses are short between cues (about 2–3s) and long after pointers (10–60s). Silence grows toward the end, with the longest pause often coming at the close. In longer sessions, silence takes up most of the runtime.
