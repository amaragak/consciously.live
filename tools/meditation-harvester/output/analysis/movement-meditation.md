# Movement meditation

The sources fall into two families. The qigong and mindful-movement routines (about seven) are instruction-dense and continuous. They give a movement cue, pair it with a breath cue and a brief rationale, repeat it ("one more time"), then slow down and close with a centering move. The walking meditations (about eight) are sparse attention-guides. They move through the body from feet upward, then widen to the senses and the surroundings, then come back to the breath, and they leave long silences for the listener to walk in. Two Thich Nhat Hanh-style sources are mostly talk, with breath-and-step phrases and long teaching asides. They fit weakly and should not set the default tone. The 7-minute qigong video and the 20-minute seniors' video are the least meditative. One source stops speaking and plays music for 5 minutes or more.

Shared across sources: an immediate welcome with no long preamble; a posture or stance cue; breath synchronised to movement; naming each movement before giving it; body parts addressed in sequence; a "slow it down, back to center" wind-down; and a gratitude or carry-it-forward close.

Pauses: movement routines use short pauses of 1–3s between cues, because the voice carries the movement. Walking practices use 5–60s and sometimes minutes, because the listener is doing the task. Silence does not grow smoothly over a session, but the longest gaps cluster in the middle (the walking stretch) and just before the close. Pause length follows the task, not the clock.
