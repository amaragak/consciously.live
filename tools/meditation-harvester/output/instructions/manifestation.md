> Guidance for Manifestation meditations, not fixed rules. Use judgement for each moment; the user's request overrides any of this.

## Mechanism
Manifestation moves the listener from a tense, wanting state into a relaxed one, then holds a desired future in imagination until it feels already true in the body. The felt state is the product, not the idea of the goal. You get there by:
- Relaxing first, so the mind is receptive.
- Making the outcome concrete through the senses.
- Locating the emotion in the body.
- Anchoring it with repetition.
- Releasing the wish, so wanting turns into trust.

Phrase the destination as already true or certain. Hedges leave the listener in "hoping".

## Phases
Default arc: arrive → settle and deepen → intention → future scene → feeling in the body → anchor → surrender → gratitude and return. Fold or split these to fit the request.

- **Arrive and settle (about 10–15%).** Welcome, posture, a few breaths, then a body or descending-imagery deepening.
- **Intention (short).** Name or invite the specific wish. When the user supplied a goal, name it here.
- **Future scene (the longest, about 30–40%).** Build in this order: where you are, what you see and hear, who is there, then how it feels.
- **Embodiment and anchor (about 15–20%).** Breathe the emotion into the body. Use affirmations or a phrase to seal it in.
- **Surrender (about 10%).** Hand the wish over and trust the timing.
- **Gratitude and return (about 10%).** Thank, deepen once, then come back.

A phase ends when the technique or focus changes. Longer sessions add depth and silence to the scene and embodiment phases more than they add new phases; they may add a second scene (a day, then a life) or a belief-release phase. Carry one thread through every section: the user's named wish and its dominant feeling. Reuse a small set of images and one anchor phrase. End each section on a line that the next can pick up ("…and from here, let the picture come into focus").

## How to say it
- **Cue anatomy:** one thing to attend to, plus a soft verb, plus an open detail. For example, "Notice what you can see around you… let the details fill in." Prefer open questions ("What are you wearing?") during the scene. They invite the listener to fill it in themselves.
- **Sequence a scene:** setting, then senses, then other people, then emotion, then body location. Give each sense its own cue and its own pause.
- **Descending imagery:** use steps, light moving down, or sinking, for deepening. Use it to enter the scene.
- **Frame as true:** "You are already here." Use "as if" only to enter the scene, then drop it.
- **Soften:** "allow", "let", "you may notice". Do not push or command.
- **Repetition:** return to one short anchor line several times, varying one word each time. Do not use a long list of affirmations.
- **Plain language:** sensory words, short sentences, "you".
- **Weave in the user's material:** use their own words for the goal, their named people and places, and their stated doubts. Where they name a block, acknowledge it once and release it on the exhale.

## Pauses
A pause gives the listener time to do what the line asked. A short cue with a quick action takes a few seconds. A cue that asks them to imagine or feel takes 10–30s. Silence grows as the session deepens, so allow the longest holds in the scene and embodiment. Use shorter pauses toward the return. Do not pause mid-sentence or between a cue and its own continuation. Write pauses as [[PAUSE Ns]].

## Opening and closing
- **Open** with a brief welcome and settling breaths. Add posture only if it helps. Name the intention.
- **Close** by releasing the wish, thanking, a final deepening breath, then gentle return and eyes open. When asked for a morning use, add a line about carrying the feeling into the day. When asked for sleep, replace the return with drifting off.

## What listeners expect
- Most sessions open with a welcome and settling breaths. Many add an intention, body arrival, or posture. Most close by deepening once more and giving gratitude. Many then return attention and open the eyes. Some carry the feeling into the day.
- Delivery is soothing, fairly assertive and certain, in "you" voice, in as-if-true tense. Questions are rare to occasional, and repetition is common. Some sessions invite the listener to repeat a line.
- Phrasing that fits:
  - descending or deepening ("down through your…")
  - relaxing and releasing
  - abundance and appreciation
  - inner world
  - noticing change ("you find yourself…")
  - self-worth and love
  - calm and safety
  - warmth or energy in the body
- Keep rare: generic body-part locating, attention invitations, and spine-path energy.
- Avoid: wishes for others' health, safety, or happiness (loving-kindness), arm and hand placement cues (movement), and "I am open to receive" loops (affirmation-loop).

## Common mistakes
- Staying abstract. Fix it by asking for sights, sounds, and sensations.
- Teaching the law of attraction at length. A line or two is enough, so keep the time for practice.
- Hedged language ("might", "hope to"). State it as true.
- Too many affirmations in a row. Choose one anchor and vary it.
- Skipping surrender. End by releasing, not gripping.
- Pausing the same length everywhere.

## Worked example
**Request:** "10-minute morning meditation to feel confident about my job interview next week."

**Phases:**
1. Arrive and settle: breaths and descending relaxation.
2. Intention: the interview, and the feeling "steady".
3. Scene: walk in, the room, the interviewer, the voice.
4. Embody: steadiness in the chest, breathed in.
5. Anchor and surrender: one phrase, then trust.
6. Thank and return.
Carried across: the "steady" feeling and the hand on the chest.

**Excerpt (scene):**
You walk into the room, and notice how easily your feet find the floor. [[PAUSE 6s]]
Look across the table. What do you see on the face of the person waiting for you? [[PAUSE 12s]]
Your voice comes out clear and unhurried. Hear the first sentence. [[PAUSE 10s]]
Now feel it in your chest: steady, warm, already yours. [[PAUSE 20s]]
