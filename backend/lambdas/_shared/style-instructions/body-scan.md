> Guidance for Body scan meditations, not fixed rules. Use judgement for each moment; the user's request overrides any of this.

# Body scan: writer's brief

Everything here is a default. If the request asks for something different (order, length, focus, tone, imagery), follow the request and use this only for what it leaves open.

## 1. Mechanism
A body scan trains attention to land on one region at a time and simply feel it. Relaxation is a by-product. Three things do the work:
- **A narrow, moving spotlight.** One region at a time gives the wandering mind a concrete, bounded task.
- **Sensation over story.** Cues point to what is felt (warmth, pressure, tingling, nothing at all), not to how it should feel.
- **Permission.** Telling the listener nothing is required lowers effort, and effort is what keeps tension in place.

Judge any new request by asking whether it keeps attention small, felt and unforced.

## 2. Phases
Divide by change of focus or technique:
1. **Arrival** (~10–15%): posture, eyes, a few settling breaths, and what the practice is and isn't.
2. **Breath anchor** (~10%): one steady sensation to return to.
3. **The scan** (~55–65%): the sweep through the body. Split it into 2–4 sections by territory (for example lower body, torso, arms, head). A longer session slows each region and adds more sub-parts; it does not add new kinds of phase.
4. **Whole-body widening** (~10–15%): attention opens from parts to one field. Give this the most silence.
5. **Return** (~5%): breath, small movements, eyes open, a line to carry forward.

**Carry-through.** Keep one handoff pattern across the scan, for example "on the out-breath, let this region go, and move to…". Keep one thread, such as acceptance or the user's theme. End each section on the region it covered so the next one can open from it ("from the hips, up into the lower back…").

**Default direction.** Sweep in one consistent direction (feet-up, or head-down if the request suggests it), then widen to the whole.

## 3. How things are said
**Anatomy of a cue.** Name the region, invite with a soft verb, leave the outcome open: "Bring your attention to your left foot… whatever sensations are there, or none." Optionally follow with one gentle release invitation.
- **Sequence within a region** from surface to depth, or from the largest part to the smallest, with one detail per cue.
- **Vary the cue.** Alternate noticing ("what's here?"), breathing ("imagine the breath reaching the knee") and letting go. Repeating one form makes the scan mechanical.
- **Repeat the frame, not the sentence.** Reuse the handoff and acceptance lines, but vary the verbs.
- **Soften instructions** with "you might", "perhaps", "if it feels right", "as best you can". Include "blank or subtle is fine".
- **Wandering mind.** Say once or twice that drifting is normal, then invite a return to the region or breath.
- **Voice.** Plain, second person, present tense, short sentences, concrete sensory words. Use few questions, and make them quiet.
- **Weaving the user's material.** Put their stress, pain or goal where the body would hold it. Use the part they name, and hold any struggle with acceptance rather than fixing: "if your shoulders are carrying this week, just notice them." Don't promise outcomes.

## 4. Pauses
Write as [[PAUSE Ns]]. A pause gives time to do what the line asked. A short cue to notice a region takes a few seconds; an invitation to feel takes longer. Typical gaps are 2–3s, and longer ones of 10–20s fall after a region's main cue. Silence lengthens as the session deepens, and the whole-body rest and the close can hold the longest. Don't pause mid-phrase or between a cue and its softener.

## 5. Opening and closing
**Open** by welcoming, then posture, eyes, settling breaths, arriving in the body, and a line of permission. Keep it brief for short sessions.

**Close** by deepening once more with a whole-body rest, then returning the breath, small movements, eyes open, and an optional gratitude or carry-forward line. Fit to the request (a sleep brief can skip the return).

## What listeners expect
- **Opening:** most sessions: posture, then settling breaths, body arrival and permission/acceptance. Many include a welcome. About half name an intention.
- **Closing:** most deepen once more, then return attention. Some add movement, eyes open, gratitude, or carrying the practice into the day.
- **Delivery:** soft, permissive, soothing. Second person, present tense, few questions, moderate repetition, rarely asking the listener to repeat lines.
- **Fitting phrasing:** whole-body awareness ("your whole body"); accepting experience as it is ("no right way to feel"); awareness travelling ("down through the body").
- **Keep rare:** generic relaxing language such as "relax and let go" or "deeper and deeper".
- **Avoid:** loving-kindness wishes and naming people (loving-kindness); hand and arm positioning cues (movement meditation).

## 7. Common mistakes
- Prescribing what the listener should feel, or saying "relax" at every region.
- Rushing every region at the same pace.
- Dropping the whole-body widening.
- Stacking imagery on the scan; it competes with sensation.
- Using the user's theme as a lecture instead of a thread.
- Too many questions.

## 8. Worked example
**Request:** "15-minute scan for tension after work, I hold it in my shoulders and jaw."

**Phases:**
1. Arrival, with permission to feel however you feel.
2. Breath anchor.
3. Scan, lower body up to hips. Slower.
4. Scan, torso, arms, then shoulders and jaw, given extra time.
5. Whole-body rest.
6. Return.

**Carries:** the out-breath handoff and the "just notice" thread, with the day's tension named lightly.
