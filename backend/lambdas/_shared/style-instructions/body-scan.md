> Guidance for Body scan meditations, not fixed rules. Use judgement for each moment; the user's request overrides any of this.

# Body scan: writing guide

## Mechanism
The listener's attention moves like a slow spotlight, one region at a time. Naming a place gives attention a target. Silence lets sensation appear. A soft release cue lets tension drop without effort. The writing must narrow attention gently, stay non-forcing, and make every result acceptable, including "nothing here". If the listener feels judged or rushed, the style fails.

## Phases
Default arc (sections are split where focus or technique changes):
1. **Arrival** (short): posture, permission to adjust, a few breaths. Set the tone: nothing to achieve.
2. **Anchor**: attention on the breath or on contact with the surface, so the scan starts from something felt.
3. **Scan** (the longest block, over half the session): move through the body in one continuous direction. Split it into 2–4 sections by territory (e.g. lower body, torso, arms, head). Each section starts and ends on a clear handover.
4. **Integration**: widen to the whole body as one field; rest without instruction.
5. **Return**: movement, orientation, eyes open.

Longer sessions deepen each region and add a mid-scan reminder about wandering attention. They do not add phases. Short sessions group regions together ("feet and ankles"). Carry across: one consistent direction, a recurring release phrase, and one breath-linked technique. The last line of each section should name where attention rests now, so the next section can pick up from there.

## Saying it
- **Cue anatomy:** name the area, invite noticing, leave the outcome open. Example: "Bring attention to your left foot. Whatever is here, tingling, warmth, or nothing at all."
- **Sequence per region:** arrive, notice, optionally breathe with it, release on an out-breath, then hand over. Vary the release wording so it doesn't become a mechanical loop.
- **Softeners:** "if you like", "you might notice", "see if". Use these rather than commands, and add "no right way to feel".
- **Language:** plain, concrete, sensory (pressure, warmth, weight, contact). Present tense, second person, short sentences. Avoid anatomy lectures and medical claims.
- **Tension-prone areas** (jaw, shoulders, lower back) can get a short, non-alarming note.
- **Wandering mind:** normalise it and return gently, without scolding.
- **User context:** use their goal or difficulty to choose emphasis, such as lingering at the area they hold stress, or framing the release in their words. Do not turn the scan into a story or add imagery unless they ask.
- **Variants:** compassion framing, grounding, or sleep focus are lenses on the same cue structure. Use them only if requested.

## Pauses
A pause gives time to do what the line asked. Writing it as [[PAUSE Ns]]: short beats of about 2–4s between linked cues; about 8–15s after an area invitation or release; longer in the integration and before the close. Silence tends to lengthen as the session deepens. Don't pause mid-sentence or between a cue and its softener.

## Opening and closing
Open briefly: settle, breathe, and drop any lecture. Adapt to the request (seated, lying, sleep, quick reset). Close by widening to the whole body, then re-orienting: fingers and toes, sounds, eyes. For sleep requests, end by fading out instead of re-energising.

## Mistakes
- Rushing through regions with no silence; give every region its own pause.
- "Relax!" commands that create effort; invite instead.
- Jumping around the body; keep one direction.
- Over-explaining physiology or benefits.
- Repeating identical cues verbatim in every region.
- Mood-dependent imagery that overrides the sensation focus.

## Worked example
Request: "10 minutes, I carry stress in my neck and shoulders after desk work."

Phases:
1. Arrival and breath (≈2 min): settle, feel contact.
2. Scan from the feet upward (≈4 min): brisk through legs and torso, handing over on "attention now rests in the belly".
3. Linger on the shoulders, neck, jaw (≈3 min): the user's focus, with the most silence.
4. Whole-body rest and return (≈1 min).

Excerpt from phase 3:
Now let attention rise to your shoulders. [[PAUSE 3s]]
Notice what's there: weight, tightness, or maybe very little. [[PAUSE 8s]]
On your next out-breath, see if they can drop a little, only as much as they want to. [[PAUSE 10s]]
Let that go, and bring awareness to the back of your neck. [[PAUSE 8s]]
