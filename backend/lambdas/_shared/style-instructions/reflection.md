> Guidance for Reflection meditations, not fixed rules. Use judgement for each moment; the user's request overrides any of this.

# Reflection meditation: writing guide

## Mechanism
Reflection turns attention inward onto the listener's own life, by asking and then leaving room to answer. The listener stops listening and starts thinking, remembering or feeling, and the voice acts as a gentle companion. What you write sets the topic and invites the answer, and the silence that follows holds the answer. So keep the lines few and the silences meaningful. Never answer for the listener.

## Phases
Divide by change of task. The usual kinds:
- **Arrival**: settle, breathe, release effort (short).
- **Body anchor**: one or two sensory cues so reflection happens from feeling, not just thought.
- **Opening the theme**: name what today is about and ask the first easy question.
- **Deepening inquiry**: the longest phase. Questions move from surface to underneath (what / where in the body / what is it asking for).
- **Response**: offer something back, such as a phrase, a hand on the heart, or forgiveness. Reflection should end in some small kindness, not a diagnosis.
- **Rest and noticing**: "What's different now?"
- **Return**: breath, a carried-forward intention, a thanks.

Arrival and return are each roughly a tenth of the session. Inquiry takes about half. Longer sessions usually add more rounds of inquiry (another memory, a second direction, a wider circle) rather than stretching one question. Shorter ones compress the arrival and use one thread.

Carry one thread through every section, such as the user's theme, a recurring image or a repeated question stem. Each section should end on a settled image or an open question, and the next should pick it up in its first line ("Staying with that feeling…"). Default arc: settle, ground, ask, deepen, offer, notice, return.

## How to say it
- **Cue anatomy**: name the focus plainly, invite gently, leave the answer open. Example: "Bring to mind one moment this week you felt quietly grateful. Let it come, whatever it is."
- **Sequence** questions from easy to tender: recall, then feel, then locate in the body, then ask what is needed. One question at a time. Questions the listener can answer in any way work better than yes/no ones.
- **Soften** with "you might", "see if", "perhaps", "if it's available". Offer an alternative when something may be hard: "If that's too raw, choose something smaller."
- **Repeat** a stem ("What do you notice…") or a short phrase the listener can adopt, and vary its ending. Keep phrase lists short, a few lines at most.
- Use second person, present tense, short plain sentences and sensory words (chest, throat, warmth). Avoid jargon and sweeping claims.
- **User material**: reflect the user's own words and situation in the questions (their goal, struggle or person). Weave them in; don't list them. Example: "What would the part of you that's been carrying this deadline most like to hear?"
- **Transitions** are one line that names the shift: "Now let's turn toward what you need."

## Pauses
A pause gives time for what the line asked, so match length to the task. Short breath or body cues need about 2–3s. Questions and "remember…" prompts need longer, about 8–30s, and occasionally more in the deepest section. Silence tends to lengthen as the session deepens and shortens toward the return. ## Opening and closing
Open with a brief welcome, name the theme in a sentence, and move quickly into breath. Close by returning to breath and body, naming what the listener did, offering one thing to carry forward, and ending warmly with no new material. Adapt both to the request, and if the user wants a sleep or evening ending, make the return slower.

## Common mistakes
- Stacking questions without silence. Ask one and wait.
- Telling the listener what they should feel or conclude. Invite instead.
- Turning it into affirmation after affirmation, or lecturing. Questions lead, and statements stay brief.
- Going straight to the hardest material. Warm up with ease first.
- Forgetting the user's details. Name them.
- Ending without a response step. Give the listener something kind to leave with.

## Worked example
Request: "10 minutes, I keep replaying a tense meeting and feel like I failed."

Phases:
1. Arrival: breath, shoulders.
2. Locate the replay in the body.
3. Inquiry: "What am I telling myself?", then "What would I say to a friend?"
4. Response: a hand on the heart and one kind sentence.
5. Return.

The thread throughout is the meeting, and the question "what's true here?" returns in each section.

Excerpt (inquiry):
"Let the meeting come back, just for a moment. [[PAUSE 6s]] Notice where it lands in your body. [[PAUSE 8s]] Now ask quietly, what am I telling myself about it? [[PAUSE 20s]] If a friend had been in that room, what would you want them to know? [[PAUSE 25s]]"
