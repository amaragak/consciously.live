When your mind won’t stop wandering

5 min read·Practice·Updated Oct 2026

A wandering mind isn’t a sign you’re doing it wrong. It’s the thing you’re practising with. Every time you notice you’ve drifted and come back, that’s the rep.

Why the mind wanders

Your brain is built to scan for what’s next: problems to solve, conversations to rehearse, things you forgot. Sitting still doesn’t switch that off. If anything, the quiet makes it louder for a while.

That’s not a failure of willpower. It’s the default setting, and meditation is simply the practice of noticing it.

“The moment you notice you’ve wandered, you’re already back.”

What to do when it happens

Most people try to stop the thoughts. That rarely works, and it tends to add a layer of frustration on top. A gentler approach works better:

Try this · 2 minutes
1NoticeWhen you realise you’re somewhere else, pause. No need to rewind how you got there.
2Name it lightlySay “thinking” or “planning” in your head. One word, no judgement.
3Come backReturn to the next breath, as if it’s the first one you’ve noticed today.

Make it easier on yourself

Shorter sits help. Five focused minutes beat twenty spent fighting. A voice or a soft soundscape gives the mind something to rest on, and body scans are often easier than breath focus when you’re restless.

✦Key idea: attention is a muscle, and wandering is the resistance it trains against.
